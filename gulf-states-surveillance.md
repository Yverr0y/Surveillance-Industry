### Petrodollar Surveillance Shopping Spree

The UAE and Saudi Arabia have used their vast oil wealth to become the world's most prolific buyers of foreign surveillance technology. Rather than building domestic spyware industries, they import from Israel, Europe, and recruit Western intelligence operatives. The targeting pattern is consistent: journalists, activists, dissidents, rival royals, and foreign diplomats. The Khashoggi murder brought global attention, but the surveillance apparatus runs far deeper.

## UAE - DarkMatter & Successors

### DarkMatter
`UAE` `Exposed` `Offensive Cyber`

- **Location:** Abu Dhabi, UAE
- **Founded:** ~2014
- **Program:** Project Raven — recruited former NSA and CIA hackers to build offensive cyber capabilities for UAE intelligence. Developed "Karma," an iPhone hacking tool exploiting iMessage vulnerabilities for zero-click access.
- **Targets:** Journalists (including at Al Jazeera), human rights activist Ahmed Mansoor (now serving 10-year prison sentence), foreign diplomats, rival governments, and political dissidents.
- **Exposed:** Reuters investigation in 2019 exposed Project Raven. Former NSA operatives Marc Baier, Ryan Adams, and Daniel Gericke were charged by DOJ and paid $1.68M in fines (2021 deferred prosecution agreement).
- **Rebranded:** Operations folded into Edge Group and Digital14, UAE state defense conglomerates that continue cyber operations under new names.

### Group 42 (G42)
`UAE` `AI` `Facial Recognition`

- **Location:** Abu Dhabi, UAE
- **Product:** AI analytics, facial recognition, data processing at national scale. Operates TOD (Technology, Operations, and Data) platform.
- **Chinese Links:** Previously partnered with Huawei on AI development. Used Chinese surveillance tech infrastructure. Processed COVID genomic data through BGI (Chinese state-linked genomics company).
- **US Pivot:** Microsoft invested $1.5B (2024). G42 agreed to divest Chinese holdings as condition. Now positioning as US-aligned AI company despite surveillance origins.
- **Notable:** Chaired by Sheikh Tahnoon bin Zayed Al Nahyan (UAE National Security Adviser). The company sits at the intersection of AI, surveillance, and Gulf state power.

### ToTok
`UAE` `Exposed` `Mass Surveillance`

- **Location:** Abu Dhabi, UAE
- **Front Company:** Breej Holding, linked to DarkMatter
- **Product:** Free messaging app marketed as alternative to blocked services (WhatsApp/Skype restricted in UAE). In reality, a mass surveillance tool created by UAE intelligence.
- **Collected:** Real-time location data, contacts, calendar, photos, messages, and conversations from every user. Millions of downloads before exposure.
- **Exposed:** New York Times investigation (December 2019) revealed ToTok was a UAE intelligence tool. Removed from Apple App Store and Google Play. Briefly returned before permanent removal.

### Israeli Vendors via UAE Entities
`UAE` `Israeli Tech`

- **Context:** The Abraham Accords (2020) formalized Israel-UAE relations, but covert surveillance cooperation predates normalization by years.
- **Operations:** Various Israeli surveillance companies (Candiru, NSO Group, Circles Technologies) operate through UAE-based entities or sell directly to UAE intelligence services.
- **Notable:** UAE was among the first Gulf states to deploy Pegasus. The normalization of Israel-Gulf relations was partly driven by shared surveillance interests and technology transfer.

## Saudi Arabia

### Pegasus Deployment (NSO Group)
`Saudi` `Exposed` `Spyware`

- **Spend:** Kingdom spent approximately $55M on NSO Group's Pegasus spyware.
- **Khashoggi Connection:** Saudi Arabia used Pegasus to spy on Jamal Khashoggi's inner circle before his murder in the Saudi consulate in Istanbul (October 2, 2018). Khashoggi was dismembered. The phone of his fiancée Hatice Cengiz was targeted.
- **Other Targets:** Omar Abdulaziz (Saudi YouTuber and activist based in Canada), Saudi dissidents abroad, Al Jazeera journalists, and members of rival royal factions.
- **Scale:** Citizen Lab and Amnesty Tech identified numerous Saudi-linked Pegasus infections across multiple countries.

### Saudi Data & AI Authority (SDAIA)
`Saudi` `State Apparatus` `Mass Surveillance`

- **Location:** Riyadh, Saudi Arabia
- **Product:** Tawakkalna app — originally deployed as COVID-19 contact tracing. Evolved into permanent digital surveillance infrastructure. Required for entry to public spaces, integrated with national identity systems.
- **Capabilities:** Real-time location tracking, health data, travel permits, facial recognition integration. Effectively turned COVID response into a population monitoring platform.
- **Vision 2030:** SDAIA is central to Saudi Arabia's digital transformation, with AI and data collection embedded into all government services.

### NEOM
`Saudi` `Smart City` `Surveillance by Design`

- **Budget:** $500 billion megaproject in northwest Saudi Arabia
- **The Line:** A 170km linear city designed with surveillance embedded into the infrastructure from inception. AI monitoring of all residents. Sensors throughout. Digital twin of the entire city for real-time simulation and tracking.
- **Design Philosophy:** Every aspect of resident life — movement, energy use, health, social interaction — monitored and optimized by AI. Described as "cognitive city" that learns from its inhabitants.
- **Notable:** Represents the endpoint of surveillance urbanism: a city built from scratch where opting out of monitoring is architecturally impossible.

### Hacking Team Customer
`Saudi` `Spyware`

- **Product:** RCS (Remote Control System) spyware purchased from Italian company Hacking Team.
- **Exposed:** Hacking Team suffered a massive data breach in July 2015. Leaked emails and invoices confirmed Saudi Arabia as a customer, along with details of spyware deployment against domestic targets.
- **Context:** Saudi Arabia's Hacking Team purchase predated its Pegasus acquisition, showing a pattern of shopping for offensive cyber capabilities from multiple vendors simultaneously.

## UAE Surveillance Infrastructure

### Falcon Eye
`UAE` `CCTV` `Facial Recognition`

- **Location:** Abu Dhabi, UAE
- **System:** Citywide CCTV surveillance network covering Abu Dhabi. Thousands of cameras equipped with facial recognition and license plate readers.
- **Capabilities:** Integrated command center for real-time monitoring. Automated alerts on persons of interest. Vehicle tracking across the emirate.
- **Notable:** One of the most comprehensive urban surveillance deployments in the Middle East. Operated in coordination with Abu Dhabi Police and security services.

### Oyoon ("Eyes")
`UAE` `AI` `Predictive Policing`

- **Location:** Dubai, UAE
- **System:** Dubai's AI-powered surveillance camera network. Thousands of cameras deployed across the city with advanced analytics.
- **Claims:** Marketed as capable of predicting crime before it occurs. Behavioral analysis, anomaly detection, crowd monitoring.
- **Notable:** Part of Dubai's "Smart City" initiative. Integrates with traffic systems, public transit, and commercial areas for comprehensive urban monitoring.

### PASS (Public Authority for Social Security)
`UAE` `Identity Systems`

- **Location:** Abu Dhabi, UAE
- **System:** Comprehensive resident tracking system integrated with national identity infrastructure.
- **Capabilities:** Links identity documents, biometrics, residency status, employment, and social services into unified tracking database.
- **Notable:** In a country where ~90% of the population are foreign workers dependent on employer-sponsored visas, identity tracking systems function as labor control mechanisms.

## Regional Targeting Patterns

### Ahmed Mansoor
`UAE` `Targeted`

- **Country:** United Arab Emirates
- **Profile:** Human rights activist, poet, and blogger. One of the "UAE Five" who petitioned for democratic reform.
- **Surveillance:** Targeted simultaneously by three different spyware vendors — NSO Group (Pegasus), FinFisher, AND Hacking Team. Citizen Lab dubbed him the "million dollar dissident" due to the combined cost of spyware deployed against him.
- **Outcome:** Arrested in 2017. Sentenced to 10 years in prison for "damaging the country's unity" via social media posts. Held in solitary confinement. International calls for his release ignored.

### Jamal Khashoggi
`Saudi` `Murdered`

- **Country:** Saudi Arabia (journalist, US resident)
- **Profile:** Saudi journalist and Washington Post columnist. Critic of Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman.
- **Surveillance:** Inner circle surveilled with Pegasus spyware. Saudi intelligence monitored his communications and movements before the operation.
- **Outcome:** Murdered inside the Saudi consulate in Istanbul on October 2, 2018. Dismembered by a 15-man Saudi hit team. CIA concluded MBS "approved" the operation. The case became a watershed moment exposing the intersection of surveillance technology and state violence.

### Loujain al-Hathloul
`Saudi` `Targeted`

- **Country:** Saudi Arabia
- **Profile:** Women's rights activist who campaigned to end Saudi Arabia's male guardianship system and the ban on women driving.
- **Surveillance:** Phone hacked with Pegasus spyware. Digital surveillance was used to build the case against her and monitor her network.
- **Outcome:** Arrested in 2018. Imprisoned for nearly three years. Reported torture including waterboarding, electric shocks, and sexual harassment. Released February 2021 under travel ban. Her case helped prompt Apple to develop Lockdown Mode.

### Al Jazeera Journalists
`UAE` `Saudi` `Targeted`

- **Country:** Qatar-based, targeted by UAE and Saudi Arabia
- **Profile:** Journalists and producers at the Al Jazeera news network, which both UAE and Saudi Arabia view as hostile.
- **Surveillance:** 36+ phones of Al Jazeera journalists and executives hacked using NSO Group's Pegasus spyware. Exploited an iMessage zero-click vulnerability (KISMET).
- **Exposed:** Citizen Lab published findings in December 2020, documenting one of the largest known clusters of Pegasus targeting against a single organization.

## Key Observations

**Petrodollar Surveillance Shopping:** Both UAE and Saudi Arabia treat surveillance technology as an import commodity. Unlike Israel (which builds and exports), the Gulf states are buyers — using oil wealth to purchase the most advanced spyware, recruit foreign intelligence operatives, and deploy mass monitoring systems at national scale. The result is a surveillance apparatus built from off-the-shelf components sourced from Israel, Europe, and the United States.

**Targeting Pattern:** The targets are consistent across both countries: journalists (Khashoggi, Al Jazeera), human rights activists (Mansoor, al-Hathloul), political dissidents abroad (Omar Abdulaziz), rival royals, and foreign diplomats. The surveillance is not primarily about national security — it is about regime preservation and silencing criticism.

**Khashoggi as Watershed Moment:** The murder of Jamal Khashoggi in October 2018 became the defining case linking surveillance technology to physical violence. Pegasus was used to monitor his inner circle, intelligence gathered through spyware informed the operation, and a Washington Post columnist was killed inside a diplomatic facility. The case forced global scrutiny of the spyware industry and Gulf state surveillance practices.

**Surveillance Embedded in Megaprojects:** NEOM and "The Line" represent the next evolution — surveillance not bolted onto existing cities but architecturally integrated from the foundation. A $500B city where every resident is monitored by default, where opting out is physically impossible. This is the logical endpoint of Gulf surveillance ambitions: a built environment designed for total awareness.

**Western Intelligence Recruitment:** DarkMatter's Project Raven demonstrated that Gulf states don't just buy technology — they recruit the people who built it. Former NSA and CIA operatives were hired to run offensive cyber operations against targets including US persons. The DOJ prosecution was a slap on the wrist ($1.68M in fines, no prison time), sending a weak deterrent signal.
