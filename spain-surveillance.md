### Europe's Short-Lived Offensive Shop Cluster

Spain is usually discussed as a **buyer** of spyware (the Catalonia Pegasus scandal). Less covered: Spain also hosted a small cluster of **offensive cyber / spyware startups** that sold zero-day capability and government spyware — until regulatory pressure, staff flight, and financing problems closed them in 2024–2025. The Spanish dossier is therefore half exporter, half cautionary tale about how quickly the European mercantile spyware layer can appear and disappear.

## Offensive / Spyware Vendors

### Variston IT
`zero-day` `spyware` `shutdown`

- **Location:** Spain
- **Product:** Offensive cyber capability and spyware tooling marketed to government clients; associated in industry reporting with high-end browser and OS exploit work.
- **Notable:** Secretive European spyware startup. Google TAG and other research groups tracked commercial spyware vendors as a major source of zero-days against Chrome and related products. Trade and tech press (2024–2025) reported Variston losing staff and effectively shutting down amid the broader European crackdown and financing squeeze on mercenary spyware firms.

### Mollitiam Industries
`spyware` `cyber-intelligence` `bankrupt`

- **Location:** Toledo / Madrid area, Spain
- **Product:** Cyber-intelligence and spyware tools supplied to government clients, including longstanding work for Spanish government buyers (per Intelligence Online and TechCrunch reporting).
- **Notable:** Filed for bankruptcy (January 2025) and shut down after cumulative financial and market pressure. Intelligence Online called it Spain's cyberoffensive leader forced to close despite a domestic legal climate long described as friendly to the sector. Demonstrates that even "home-market" spyware vendors are not immune once capital, talent, and export risk turn.

## Import / State Use (Context)

### CNI (Centro Nacional de Inteligencia)
`state` `sigint` `domestic-foreign`

- **Role:** Spain's national intelligence service.
- **Notable:** Central to Spain's dual role as both host of offensive vendors and documented Pegasus customer environment. The Catalonia spyware scandal (journalists, politicians, civil society) put Spanish government use of imported NSO tooling under global scrutiny — separate from Variston/Mollitiam as exporters, but part of the same national surveillance picture.

### Catalonia Pegasus Scandal
`pegasus` `import` `political-targeting`

- **Summary:** Citizen Lab and media investigations documented Pegasus infections among Catalan independence figures, lawyers, and related targets — one of Europe's largest known spyware clusters.
- **Notable:** Shows Spain as a significant **import** market for Israeli military-grade mobile spyware even while domestic shops tried to build a European offensive alternative.

## Key Observations

**Exporter chapter closed (for now).** Variston and Mollitiam's collapse removed Spain's two most visible commercial spyware names in a single year. Talent and tooling may reappear under new shells — standard pattern in this industry.

**Buyer and seller.** Spain's importance is not only domestic shops; the Pegasus/Catalonia case makes it a core European case study in democratic spyware abuse.

**Regulatory climate matters.** Trade press long described Spanish law as relatively hospitable to cyberoffensive business — until market and reputational pressure closed the leaders anyway.
