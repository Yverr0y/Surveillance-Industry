Company Overview

What Palantir Does

Palantir is a data integration and analytics company that builds software enabling organizations to integrate, manage, and analyze massive amounts of data from disparate sources into unified, searchable systems.

The company's software links databases that were never meant to be connected - IRS records, immigration files, passport data, social media, license plate readers, biometrics - into comprehensive profiles on individuals.

Core capability: "The true civil liberties nightmare of Palantir is that they enable governments to link data that should have never been linked."

Origin Story

Palantir emerged from the intelligence failures of 9/11. The CIA's venture capital arm, In-Q-Tel, funded the company's development after Silicon Valley VCs rejected it.

According to leaked documents, Palantir was created "through iterative collaboration between Palantir computer scientists and analysts from various intelligence agencies over the course of nearly three years."

The technology evolved from PayPal's fraud detection systems, which inspired founders to develop software for identifying suspicious patterns.

Core Products

Palantir Gotham
Defense & Intelligence Platform
The original platform, released 2008. Imports massive amounts of structured data (spreadsheets, databases) and unstructured data (images, documents) into centralized database for visualization and analysis. Supports alerts, geospatial analysis, and prediction. Built through collaboration with CIA analysts.
Users: CIA, FBI, NSA, DHS, Marines, Air Force, SOCOM, ICE, Police Departments
Palantir Foundry
Commercial Enterprise Platform
Mid-2010s evolution of Gotham for commercial enterprises. Creates "Ontology" - a digital twin connecting digital assets to real-world assets (facilities, equipment, products). Used in healthcare, manufacturing, finance. Also adopted by government for non-intel use cases.
Users: Healthcare systems, manufacturers, financial services, NHS (UK), various corporations
Palantir AIP
AI Platform
Artificial Intelligence Platform integrating large language models and generative AI into Palantir's data infrastructure. Enables natural language queries against integrated databases. Part of 2025 Army Enterprise Agreement.
Users: US Army, Federal agencies, Commercial clients
Palantir Apollo
Deployment & Operations
Continuous delivery system managing software deployment across classified, cloud, and edge environments. Enables rapid updates to Gotham/Foundry deployments in secure government networks.
Users: All Palantir government and commercial clients
Maven Smart System (MSS)
Military AI
AI-powered battlefield intelligence system. 20,000+ active users across 35 military tools. Covers 5 combatant commands: CENTCOM, EUCOM, INDOPACOM, NORTHCOM/NORAD, TRANSCOM. NATO adopted March 2025.
Users: US Army, 5 Combatant Commands, NATO (as of 2025)
ImmigrationOS
ICE Deportation Platform
$30M contract (April 2025) to track "self-deportations" and identify priority deportation cases. Designed to: (1) streamline identification of removal targets, (2) track self-deportations with "near real-time visibility," (3) optimize deportation logistics. Prototype due Sept 2025.
Users: ICE
HHS Protect
COVID-19 Response
COVID-19 data tracking dashboard built for HHS during pandemic. Aggregated hospital capacity, case counts, and resource allocation data. Gave Palantir access to sensitive health data across the country. Part of broader push into healthcare sector.
Users: HHS, White House COVID Task Force
Tiberius
Vaccine Distribution
Vaccine distribution optimization system during COVID-19 rollout. Managed logistics of where vaccines should be sent based on population, storage requirements, and demand. Demonstrated Palantir's logistics capabilities to government.
Users: HHS, CDC, Operation Warp Speed

ICE Immigration Enforcement Tools

Palantir's Deportation Infrastructure

Since 2013, Palantir has provided ICE with systems used in workplace raids, large-scale enforcement operations, and investigations involving asylum seekers. Total ICE contracts: $257M+

Investigative Case Management (ICM)
Case management system for ICE built on Gotham. Logs and manages criminal and civil investigation files. Used by HSI, Enforcement and Removal Operations, Office of Professional Responsibility, and ICE attorneys.

Data integrated: Family relationships, immigration history, employment history, biometric identification, license plate readers, social media profiles, contact information.
FALCON
Analytical tool that ingests data from ICM and other ICE systems. Used to target people and plan raids. Until 2022, tracked agents' and targets' locations during operations, recorded information from encounters in real-time, searched federal and private databases.

Connected to: Student Exchange Visitors Program (tracking foreign students), ICEGangs database (criticized for triggering deportations on tenuous gang evidence).

Used in: 2019 Mississippi raids (680 arrested in one day), 2018 and 2016 workplace raids, ongoing enforcement operations.
ImmigrationOS (2025)
New $30M AI-driven platform to identify deportation targets, track self-deportations with "near real-time visibility," and optimize deportation logistics. Prototype due September 2025, contract through 2027.

Capabilities: Links IRS data, immigration records, private databases. Prioritizes visa overstays and "violent criminals." Enables "deportation by algorithm."

Notable incident: Mississippi raids (Aug 7, 2019) arrested 680 people in one day using Palantir systems. Children left alone for 8 days after both parents detained. Families torn apart.

Controversies & Criticism

Immigration Enforcement & Family Separation
Palantir tools have powered ICE workplace raids that separated families. The 2019 Mississippi raids arrested 680 people in one day; children were left alone for 8 days after both parents were detained. Civil liberties advocates warn of "deportation by algorithm" with little transparency or due process.
Predictive Policing & Racial Bias
Multiple police departments have used Gotham for predictive policing. The ACLU and other civil liberties organizations criticize this use for potentially reinforcing algorithmic biases that contribute to increased racial profiling and disproportionate policing of minority communities.
NSA Mass Surveillance (Snowden)
Edward Snowden's 2013 revelations included evidence that Palantir created a "helper tool" for the NSA's XKEYSCORE program - one of the most invasive surveillance systems ever deployed against civilian populations worldwide.
Former Employees Condemn Company
Former Palantir workers (including engineers, managers, and a privacy/civil liberties team member) published an open letter condemning the company's work with Trump administration. They stated the company's principles of protecting the vulnerable "have now been violated and are rapidly being dismantled."
Israel Defense Forces Partnership
In 2024, Palantir partnered with the IDF during military operations in Gaza. Human rights organizations criticized Palantir's advanced surveillance and targeting technologies amid allegations of disproportionate force against Palestinian civilians.
NHS Patient Data (UK)
In March 2023, NHS hospitals were ordered to share patient data with Palantir. Civil liberties groups (Doctors' Association UK, National Pensioners' Convention, Just Treatment) threatened legal action, criticizing Palantir for "buying their way in" to healthcare data.
Stephen Miller Conflict of Interest
Trump advisor Stephen Miller has a financial stake in Palantir while shaping immigration policies that Palantir tools enforce. The Project on Government Oversight flagged this as a conflict of interest.
"Deportation by Algorithm"
Critics argue Palantir systems enable mass deportation with no transparency. The company doesn't police use of its technology - clients decide how rigorously to enforce privacy controls. "When you combine all those data points into one database, it creates a digital ID. It's a power that history says will eventually be abused."

Palantir's Defense

Company Statements

"Palantir is committed to providing transparency around who we are and what we do."

"Since our founding, we have always placed the preservation of privacy and civil liberties at the center of our mission."

Key claim: Palantir doesn't collect data, doesn't store data, and doesn't sell data. It just enables organizations to make better use of their own data.

Reality: The company doesn't police the use of its technology. It is up to the client to decide how rigorously they enforce privacy controls. The concern has always been that technology this powerful lends itself to abuse.
