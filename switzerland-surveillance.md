### Neutrality as Cover Story

Switzerland sold the world an image of alpine neutrality and bank secrecy. For decades it also sold **Crypto AG** encryption machines secretly owned by the **CIA and West German BND** — letting Western intelligence read the traffic of more than a hundred governments. That operation (exposed by the Washington Post and ZDF in 2020) is the landmark Swiss surveillance story. Today the Swiss niche is smaller but real: **IMSI catchers**, mobile-network interception, and signaling tools still exported under SECO licenses.

## Historic: Crypto AG

### Crypto AG (Operation Rubicon / Thesaurus)
`cia` `bnd` `encryption-backdoor` `historic`

- **Location:** Steinhausen / Zug region, Switzerland (liquidated 2018)
- **Product:** Government-grade encryption machines for secure communications — marketed as Swiss neutral tech to ministries, militaries, and intelligence services in 120+ countries.
- **Notable:** Secretly owned and controlled by the CIA in partnership with Germany's BND for decades. Customers encrypted their traffic on compromised machines; Western partners could decrypt it. Products remained in use in multiple countries after liquidation. Swiss officials faced questions about how much they knew; parliamentary and criminal inquiries followed the 2020 exposure. The single most important "neutrality brand as intelligence front" case in modern history.

## Mobile Interception / Telecom

### NeoSoft AG
`imsi-catcher` `mobile-intercept` `exporter`

- **Location:** Zurich, Switzerland
- **Product:** IMSI catchers / cell-site simulators — devices that impersonate legitimate cell towers to force nearby phones to connect, revealing IMSI identifiers, location, and enabling interception of calls/SMS on vulnerable protocols.
- **Notable:** Documented in European export and procurement reporting (including high-cost deliveries to Serbia and other buyers). Part of the Swiss commercial market for mobile-phone interception that Privacy International and others tracked through SECO export licenses.

### Mitto AG
`ss7` `telecom-signaling` `messaging`

- **Location:** Switzerland
- **Product:** SS7 and telecom messaging/connectivity services — the signaling layer that carriers use to route calls and texts across borders.
- **Notable:** SS7 access is dual-use: legitimate roaming and SMS delivery, but also a classic vector for location tracking and message interception when abused or sold to the wrong end users. Swiss and European signaling providers have been scrutinized in the broader "SS7 for hire" investigative literature.

### SECO Export Regime
`export-control` `state`

- **Role:** Switzerland's State Secretariat for Economic Affairs (SECO) licenses exports of dual-use surveillance and interception equipment.
- **Notable:** Forced disclosures (Privacy International and Swiss reporting) showed multiple licenses for mobile-phone interception technology — evidence that Switzerland remains an active, regulated exporter even after Crypto AG's collapse.

## State Apparatus

### NDB (Nachrichtendienst des Bundes)
`state` `sigint` `domestic-foreign`

- **Role:** Swiss Federal Intelligence Service.
- **Notable:** Operates under post-2017 intelligence law reforms that expanded digital collection powers. Switzerland's intelligence posture is smaller than Five Eyes peers but sits on a critical European transit and finance hub.

## Key Observations

**Crypto AG is the template.** A private Swiss company, global trust, secret Western intelligence ownership — decades of readable "secure" communications. Every modern dual-use export debate in Switzerland sits in that shadow.

**IMSI / SS7 niche.** Post-Crypto Swiss industry is not NSO-scale spyware; it is mobile interception hardware and telecom plumbing sold into police and intelligence markets worldwide.

**Brand vs. product.** Neutrality and banking secrecy marketed Swiss gear as "safe from great-power spying" — which is exactly why Crypto AG worked, and why buyers still reach for Swiss-labeled interception tools.
