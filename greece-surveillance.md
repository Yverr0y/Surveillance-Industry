### Predatorgate: Europe's Spyware Capital Scandal

Greece is not a classic "Unit 8200 pipeline" exporter. It became the **European operating theater** for the **Intellexa** mercenary-spyware consortium and the site of **Predatorgate** — the EU's highest-profile democratic spyware crisis. Israeli-founded tooling (Predator, built on Cytrox tech) was procured and used against Greek journalists, opposition politicians, and others while Intellexa entities operated from Greek corporate structures. The case produced rare criminal accountability: a 2026 Athens first-instance conviction of Intellexa founder **Tal Dilian** and associates. Greece therefore belongs in this repo as both a **host jurisdiction for commercial spyware ops** and a **buyer/abuser** of the same class of tools.

## Offensive / Spyware

### Intellexa S.A. (Predator)
`predator` `mercenary-spyware` `entity-list` `eu-hub`

- **Location:** Greece (Intellexa S.A.); multi-jurisdiction consortium (Ireland, Hungary, North Macedonia, etc.)
- **Founder:** Tal Dilian (former Israeli military intelligence officer; also linked historically to other cyber-offensive ventures)
- **Product:** **Predator** — commercial mercenary spyware giving operators deep access to target phones (messages, cameras, microphones, files) after infection, often via malicious links.
- **Notable:** US Commerce **Entity List** (July 2023) added Intellexa S.A. (Greece), Intellexa Limited (Ireland), Cytrox Holdings (Hungary), and Cytrox AD (North Macedonia) for trafficking cyber exploits used to gain unauthorized access. European Parliament PEGA committee scrutinized Greece and Cyprus as hubs. Predator was documented against journalists and political targets inside Greece and exported into broader government markets. Greece is the jurisdiction where the consortium's political blowback finally hit criminal court.

### Cytrox
`predator` `north-macedonia` `hungary` `intellexa`

- **Location:** North Macedonia / Hungary origins; absorbed into the Intellexa commercial stack
- **Product:** Core Predator mobile spyware technology later marketed under the Intellexa consortium brand.
- **Notable:** Dilian acquired Cytrox as the technical engine for Predator. Entity List designations hit Cytrox entities alongside Intellexa. Product lineage is Israeli talent + Balkan/EU corporate shells — Greece became a critical sales/ops/legal node, not the sole R&D birthplace.

### Krikel / Greek procurement intermediaries
`buyer-side` `procurement` `predatorgate`

- **Location:** Greece
- **Role:** Greek commercial/security intermediaries (including firms linked to businessman **Yiannis Lavranos** in court reporting) allegedly purchased or facilitated Predator for use in the Greek market.
- **Notable:** Lavranos was among those convicted in the Athens Predatorgate case (first instance, Feb 2026) alongside Dilian, Sara Hamou, and Felix Bitzios — the local procurement link that turns foreign spyware into domestic political surveillance.

## Predatorgate Timeline (Core Facts)

### Targets & discovery
`journalists` `opposition` `eyp`

- **2022:** Financial journalist **Thanasis Koukakis** discovered Predator infection; opposition figure **Nikos Androulakis** (and others) reported targeting attempts. Parallel lawful wiretaps by the Greek National Intelligence Service (**EYP**) on journalists deepened the crisis.
- **Meta / Citizen Lab:** Confirmed Predator infections; later reporting included dual US-Greek national and former Meta security policy manager **Artemis Seaford** infected while in Greece (*New York Times*, 2023).
- **Hellenic DPA (2023):** Investigation team reported **220 Predator-polluted SMS links** sent to **92 phone numbers**.
- **Government line:** Admitted classic wiretapping of Koukakis in some contexts; denied Predator use / Intellexa association — contradicted by technical findings and subsequent prosecutions of vendor-side actors.

### Accountability
`court` `eu-precedent`

- **Oct 2022:** Koukakis sued Intellexa executives for privacy breaches.
- **2025–2026:** High-profile Athens trial of Intellexa-linked executives.
- **Feb 2026:** Athens Misdemeanour Court first-instance conviction of Tal Dilian, Sara Hamou, Felix Bitzios, and Yiannis Lavranos for unlawful access / privacy violations — rare EU criminal accountability against commercial spyware principals (sentences reported as cumulative multi-decade figures, suspended pending appeal per Amnesty/ICIJ/EDRi reporting). Civil suits by victim clusters also filed for damages.

## State Apparatus

### EYP (Ethniki Ypiresia Pliroforion)
`state` `domestic-intel` `wiretapping`

- **Role:** Greek National Intelligence Service — domestic and foreign intelligence, lawful intercept under Greek law.
- **Notable:** Central to the wiretapping half of the 2022 scandal (parallel to Predator malware). Leadership resignations and parliamentary fights followed exposure. Predatorgate blurred the line between legal wiretap authority and illegal commercial spyware use.

### ADAE / Hellenic Data Protection Authority
`oversight` `dpa`

- **Role:** Communications privacy and data-protection overseers drawn into Predatorgate investigations.
- **Notable:** Institutional friction with the executive over access to surveillance files became part of the scandal's democratic-control story — not just "spyware bad," but "who watches the watchers inside the EU."

## Key Observations

**Host, not just buyer.** Greece's importance is Intellexa/Predator *operating and being procured on EU soil*, then detonating politically inside an EU member state — not a long list of indigenous camera factories.

**Israeli talent, European shells.** Predator continues the pattern: Unit-8200-adjacent founders, multi-country corporate webs (Greece, Ireland, Hungary, North Macedonia), US Entity List pressure, and sales into authoritarian and democratic markets alike.

**Rare convictions.** Most spyware scandals end in denial and Entity List paper. Predatorgate produced named criminal convictions of vendor-linked executives — still under appeal, but a template civil society groups (Amnesty, EDRi, ICIJ) are already citing.
